# Culinary Science: The Maillard Reaction & Heat Transfer

The **Maillard Reaction** is a complex non-enzymatic chemical reaction occurring between reducing sugars and amino acids above 140°C (284°F), generating hundreds of novel flavor and aroma compounds (melanoidins).

## The Enemies of Maillard Browning
- **Moisture:** Water boils and evaporates at 100°C (212°F). Surface moisture traps pan temperature at 100°C, causing proteins to steam rather than sear. Always pat proteins thoroughly dry with paper towels and salt in advance to draw out and reabsorb moisture.
- **Crowded Pans:** Overcrowding drops pan thermal mass and traps evaporating steam.

## Heat Transfer Mechanisms
1. **Conduction:** Direct contact heat (cast iron skillet searing).
2. **Convection:** Fluid or gas circulation (convection ovens, air fryers).
3. **Radiation:** Electromagnetic waves (broilers, charcoal embers).
